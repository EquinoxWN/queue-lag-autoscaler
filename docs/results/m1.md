## Measure: the simulated bursty load (`make bench`)

Setup: in-process broker (`internal/fakebroker`), one topic with 32 partitions, each worker
processes 50 msg/s, a partition belongs to one worker, messages are served oldest first. Arrivals
are 100 msg/s with +-20% noise per second (seed 7), plus bursts of 1,500 msg/s for 60 s at 5 min,
600 msg/s for 3 min at 20 min and 3,000 msg/s for 30 s at 35 min; 50 simulated minutes in 1 s steps.
The controller ticks every 15 s with `target_drain` 2 min, 1 to 32 workers, `busy_lag` 100;
added workers start after 10 s, removed ones stop at once. The static runs keep 2 or 32 workers.

| Run | Longest wait | p99 wait | p50 wait | Worker-minutes | Peak workers | Changes | Lag at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lag-based (this tool) | 1 min 25 s | 1 min 8 s | 2 s | 227.2 | 32 | 76 | 45 |
| Static, 2 workers (baseline rate) | 27 min 18 s | 26 min 54 s | 13 min 54 s | 100.0 | 2 | 0 | 262,955 |
| Static, 32 workers (peak) | 30 s | 28 s | 1 s | 1,600.0 | 32 | 0 | 0 |

All three runs see the same 562,796 messages. Verification line:
`short_decisions=0 final_lag_ok=true max_wait_within_bound=true (bound = 2m40s)`; the demo exits
non-zero if any of these fail. Wall time of the three runs together: 40 ms.

Wait is measured per message from the step it was produced to the step a worker processed it
(1 s resolution; percentiles are whole seconds rounded down).

## Analyze

- The longest wait (85 s) stays under the 2 min target although each burst starts before the
  controller notices it: the first tick after a burst sees only part of it in the smoothed arrival
  rate, and new workers start 10 s later. The bound the demo checks (target + 2 ticks + startup)
  allows for that delay.
- The lag-based run used 14% of the worker-minutes of a fleet sized for the peak, and 2.3 times
  the minimum fleet that cannot keep up with the bursts.
- **76 changes in 50 minutes** is the visible defect: at 100 msg/s, 2 workers run at exactly their
  capacity, so with +-20% noise the lag crosses `busy_lag` now and then and the count alternates
  between 2 and 3. Stabilization windows and cooldowns are M2.
- Two design defects were found with this simulation and fixed before these numbers (ADR 0002):
  measurements taken while idle ratcheted the count from 2 to 8 at a steady rate, and a measurement
  from an interval in which workers were removed overstated per-worker capacity, so the fleet shrank
  while 9,000 messages were waiting. The first version ran the same scenario with 278.2
  worker-minutes and a longest wait of 1 min 34 s; with the fixes it is 227.2 and 1 min 25 s.

## Improve and Control

- Improvements: capacity only from busy intervals, divided by the larger worker count of the
  interval, remembered only from stable intervals; idle measurements may only lower the count.
- Control: `make ci` (gofmt, vet, `go test -race`), `make audit`, and the demo with its `verify`
  line run on every push; the demo fails the build if any check fails.

## The plan example (`make plan`)

Two `kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe` snapshots 60 s apart (`examples/`). `orders-workers`:
lag 10,600, arrivals 20 msg/s, 2 workers committed 40 msg/s while busy (20 each); with a 5 min
target it needs `20 + 10600 / 300 = 55.3` msg/s, so 3 workers, projected drain 265 s.
`emails`: lag 30, 0.5 msg/s, nothing committed in the interval, so the configured 10 msg/s per
worker is used: 1 worker. `docs/proof/plan.jpg` shows the output.
